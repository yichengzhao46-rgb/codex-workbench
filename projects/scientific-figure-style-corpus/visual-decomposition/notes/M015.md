- Legacy ID: U001

## 2. Functional role

This figure explains a proposed environmental-to-mechanistic story across several spatial and conceptual scales. It combines Late-Archean environmental context, a microbial-mat niche, mineral/semiconductor behavior, charge separation, microbial ammonium oxidation, and a thermodynamic/redox threshold in one integrated frame.

## 3. Layout and reading logic

The composition is a **scene-to-zoom multiscale layout**. The eye first reads the broad ocean/UV/volcanic environment, then the highlighted benthic mat, then the enlarged semiconductor mechanism in the center-right, and finally the redox/energy scale on the far right.

The key strength is that context and mechanism are not separated into unrelated panels: a large environmental scene acts as the visual substrate for the mechanistic callout.

## 4. Cell / object rendering

Cells and microorganisms are simplified flat icons rather than detailed anatomical renderings. Minerals are rendered as rounded particles or rocks. The semiconductor concept is deliberately abstracted into a clean band-style circular diagram rather than depicted literally as a material surface.

This is useful when the figure must bridge ecology and physical chemistry without forcing one rendering language onto every object.

## 5. Arrow grammar

Arrow colors encode process class. Charge carriers use explicit `e−` / `h+` glyphs and warm/magenta accents, while other material or biological flows use distinct arrows. The mechanism relies on directionality rather than large text blocks.

A useful lesson is to reserve the strongest accent colors for the mechanistically important electron/hole routes.

## 6. Palette / outline / typography

The palette is dominated by cool ocean blues and teals, with warm solar orange/yellow and red/magenta charge accents. This warm–cool contrast makes photochemical steps visually salient.

Typography is less disciplined than the composition: text sizes and black/white label conventions vary. The figure works despite this, not because of it.

## 7. Information density and evidence style

Information density is high. Environmental assumptions, microbial localization, material mechanism and energetic feasibility are integrated in one image. Broad background areas provide some breathing room, but the mechanistic center is dense.

## 8. Best reusable elements

- environmental panorama → mechanistic hotspot
- zoomed ecological niche
- narrow redox/energy ruler on one edge
- semiconductor/charge carrier callout
- warm–cool semantic contrast
- explicit electron/hole glyphs

## 9. Suitable future use cases in the Bath–RP project

Best suited to:

- Bath–Bio-Se environmental/mineral interface overview;
- showing an environmental niche first and an EET/EEU mechanism second;
- adding a compact redox or energy-feasibility module beside a biological mechanism;
- linking methane-rich microoxic environments to a localized material–microbe process.

## 10. Weaknesses / what not to imitate

Do not copy the mixed typography, the very high number of simultaneous concepts, or the tendency for the mechanism and environmental scene to compete for attention. For Bath figures, keep the same multiscale logic but reduce labels and use a stricter arrow/color legend.
