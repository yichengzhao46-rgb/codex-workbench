# M005

## 1. Figure identity
- Canonical ID: M005
- Legacy ID: S042
- Source type: local curated asset
- Visual class: conductive carrier + interspecies electron-transfer mechanism

## 2. Functional role
This figure connects a redox-active conductive carrier to interspecies electron-transfer behavior. Its main visual strength is combining material properties, energetic/redox compatibility and microbial electron-transfer routes in one causal frame.

## 3. Layout and reading logic
The left side introduces the carrier and a vertical potential scale. The right side shows electron-donating and methanogenic partners, with DET/DIET routes mapped between them. Reading order is material/redox feasibility → electron handoff → biological endpoint.

## 4. Cell / object rendering
Cells are soft 2.5D rods/ovals with restrained gradients. The carrier is visually distinct as a composite particle with mineral/quinone motifs. Intracellular anatomy is deliberately sparse; only the proteins/redox pools needed for the electron-transfer story are shown.

## 5. Arrow grammar
Warm red/orange arrows indicate productive electron movement. A cool blue route marks the no-DIET/alternative condition. Route labels are attached directly to the arrows, so line color and text work together to encode mechanism class.

## 6. Palette / outline / text hierarchy
Warm electron colors are balanced by cool blue alternative-route cues. Pale organism colors reduce competition with the carrier and electron arrows. The potential ruler and DET/DIET labels function as visual anchors.

## 7. Information density and evidence style
Density is high near the electron-transfer center, but the left potential ladder remains relatively open. The figure is mechanism-heavy rather than data-heavy; numeric redox values serve as embedded feasibility evidence.

## 8. Best reusable elements
- vertical redox/potential ruler;
- material-to-cell handoff;
- donor/recipient visual split;
- DET versus no-DIET color contrast;
- conductive carrier rendered as a distinct composite object.

## 9. Suitable future use cases in Bath–RP project
Highly relevant for GAC/Fe-based conductive materials, potential DIET diagrams, Bio-Se/mineral interfaces and any mechanism where the material needs to be justified both physically and electrochemically.

## 10. Weaknesses / what not to imitate
Do not draw a candidate electron route as established. Avoid crowding the recipient/donor pair with every possible cofactor or protein. The redox ladder should support the mechanism, not become a second dominant figure.
