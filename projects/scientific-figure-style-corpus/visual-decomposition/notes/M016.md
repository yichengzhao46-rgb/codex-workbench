## 2. Functional role

The figure communicates carbon reallocation under alternating aerobic and anoxic methanotrophic metabolism. Its main job is not to show detailed biochemistry; it shows **how the same biological system changes state under two contrasting environmental regimes**.

## 3. Layout and reading logic

The canvas is split vertically into a daylight/aerobic half and a night/anoxic half. A large central circular arrow visually couples the two sides and makes alternation explicit.

The intended reading order is:

1. identify the two environmental states;
2. compare the same methanotroph representation on both sides;
3. inspect changes in internal carbon allocation and released products;
4. return to the central loop to understand that the states alternate.

This is a strong **mirrored-comparison** layout because the geometry is held constant while the conditions change.

## 4. Cell / object rendering

Methanotrophs are represented by simple outlined community/cell capsules containing a few stylized internal carbon-pool symbols. No detailed membrane or protein anatomy is shown. Molecules such as CH4, CO2, O2 and H2 appear as colored floating bubbles.

The abstraction level is intentionally low-detail and presentation-friendly.

## 5. Arrow grammar

Internal black arrows carry carbon-flow meaning. The central blue circular arrow is a state-transition symbol rather than a metabolic flux. This distinction is important: the figure uses **arrow role by position and color**, not just arrow shape.

## 6. Palette / outline / typography

The condition split is encoded primarily by background color: light cyan/green for day/aerobic conditions and deep navy/blue for night/anoxic conditions. Molecule colors act as secondary accents.

Large state labels and simple typography make the concept readable at graphical-abstract scale.

## 7. Information density and evidence style

Density is medium. The design trades mechanistic detail for immediate comparison. The figure is mostly conceptual, with no embedded experimental plots.

## 8. Best reusable elements

- matched left/right condition panels
- day/night or low/high-condition environmental fields
- central state-transition loop
- consistent object geometry across conditions
- metabolite bubbles positioned outside cells
- minimal internal carbon-flow arrows

## 9. Suitable future use cases in the Bath–RP project

Best suited to:

- standard microoxic versus high-O2 perturbation;
- low-donor versus conditioned-Bath comparison;
- temporal switching between oxic and oxygen-limited states;
- showing that the same Bath-centered system reallocates outputs without implying a different organismal identity.

For the Bath–RP work, this grammar is particularly useful for a high-O2 mechanism summary if both panels preserve the same Bath/RP geometry and only the donor-output, ATP/redox and carbon-fixation consequences change.

## 10. Weaknesses / what not to imitate

Avoid decorative day/night scenery unless it carries real biological meaning. The glowing metabolite bubbles are visually friendly but can look informal if overused. A Bath–RP adaptation should keep the mirrored logic while using more restrained molecule symbols and clearer evidence-boundary notation.
