## 2. Functional role
The figure contrasts two alternative methane-production explanations under different environmental conditions. Its visual strength is the use of matched geometry to make causal differences easy to compare.

## 3. Layout and reading logic
Two panels are stacked vertically. The top uses a nighttime cue for a biotic/syntrophic route; the bottom uses a daytime cue for an abiotic/ROS-associated route. Organism positions are kept broadly comparable across panels, so the changed arrows and labels become the main message.

## 4. Cell / object rendering
Microbial groups are simplified colored clusters with small rod/cell symbols. Morphology is secondary to group identity. No detailed membrane or intracellular pathways are shown.

## 5. Arrow grammar
Black/gray arrows encode interactions and transfers. Red methane labels and inhibitory/chemical cues highlight the competing mechanism. Because the panel geometry is matched, arrows can carry most of the mechanistic contrast.

## 6. Palette / outline / text hierarchy
Yellow methanogenic clusters, green photosynthetic partners and blue oxygen cues create stable entity identities across panels. Red accents mark methane/ROS-related emphasis. Sun/moon icons serve as environmental state labels.

## 7. Information density and evidence style
Density is medium and balanced. The figure is conceptual rather than data-rich; comparison is achieved by repeating the same visual scaffold with changed routes.

## 8. Best reusable elements
- vertically stacked alternative mechanisms;
- matched organism positions across conditions;
- condition icons used as compact state labels;
- shared geometry with altered arrows to expose causal differences.

## 9. Suitable future use cases in Bath–RP project
Useful when comparing two mechanistic explanations, such as diffusible exchange versus conductive coupling, standard versus perturbation condition, or biotic versus abiotic contributions.

## 10. Weaknesses / what not to imitate
Avoid too many crossing arrows. Day/night icons should only be used when they carry mechanistic meaning. Keep panel geometry closely matched; otherwise the reader cannot isolate which mechanism actually changed.
