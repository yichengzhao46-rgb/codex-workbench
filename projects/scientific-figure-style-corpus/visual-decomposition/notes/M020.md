# M020

## 1. Figure identity

- Legacy ID: U006
- Journal: Angewandte Chemie International Edition (2026)
- Figure: Graphical Abstract
- Role in PR20: synthesis-to-function graphical-abstract reference

## 2. Functional role

This figure explains how a conductive FeS conduit is formed and then how that newly formed structure enables electron transfer. The key communication task is **causal staging**: first build the interface, then show what the interface does.

## 3. Layout and reading logic

The figure is organized as a strong left-to-right two-stage narrative.

- Left: cysteine/Fe2+-dependent formation and periplasmic FeS synthesis.
- Center: a large directional transition arrow.
- Right: the functional electron-transfer network enabled by the new conductive route.

This `process_to_function` layout is especially effective because synthesis and function are visually separated while remaining within one continuous story.

## 4. Cell / object rendering

The left side uses a simplified membrane cross-section and a few transport proteins to show Fe and sulfur entry/processing. The right side reuses the denser membrane EET grammar from M019. FeS nanoparticles/bridge elements are represented as dark bead-like particles, creating visual continuity between formation and function.

## 5. Arrow grammar

The left side relies on short black/red process arrows for precursor transport and conversion. The large center arrow is not a biochemical flux; it is a stage-transition device. The right side switches to explicit electron-flow arrows and redox loops.

This separation of **process arrows** from **story-transition arrows** is one of the most reusable features.

## 6. Palette / outline / typography

The membrane remains muted purple, while proteins and chemical inputs receive soft pastel colors. Electron routes use stronger red/blue accents. Headings under each half give the viewer a fast semantic anchor.

Typography is functional rather than decorative; the large stage titles carry more weight than individual protein labels.

## 7. Information density and evidence style

The left half is medium density; the right half is high density. The overall graphical abstract therefore has a slight imbalance, but the strong stage division keeps it interpretable.

## 8. Best reusable elements

- synthesis/assembly → functional mechanism split
- large bridging transition arrow
- repeated visual motif linking the two stages
- simple formation chemistry on the left, detailed EET on the right
- shared membrane palette across both stages
- explicit stage captions below the panels

## 9. Suitable future use cases in the Bath–RP project

Best suited to:

- Bath–Bio-Se: Se particle formation → photo/electrochemical function;
- GAC/click chemistry: interface construction → altered electron-transfer behavior;
- figures that must distinguish how a biohybrid material is created from how it later participates in EEU/EET;
- objective-overview graphics with a fabrication/formation stage followed by a mechanistic-test stage.

## 10. Weaknesses / what not to imitate

The density mismatch between the two halves should be improved in future adaptations. Avoid placing an extremely detailed molecular network beside an overly sparse synthesis panel unless the visual hierarchy is intentionally asymmetric. For Bath–Bio-Se, keep both halves closer in information weight.
