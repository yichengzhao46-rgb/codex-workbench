- Canonical ID: M011

## 2. Functional role
This graphical abstract compresses an environmental exposure story into a simple survival-versus-death narrative. It prioritizes immediate interpretation over mechanistic completeness.

## 3. Layout and reading logic
A rice-paddy scene on the left introduces CH4 oxidation and Cd exposure. The center contains a protective stress-response loop associated with cell survival. The right shows a visually broken/dead cell outcome. Reading order is strictly left-to-right.

## 4. Cell / object rendering
Cells and intracellular responses are icon-like rather than anatomically realistic. The rice/root context is simplified and serves only to localize the environmental problem. Cadmium appears as small dark dots and cell death as fragmented orange shapes.

## 5. Arrow grammar
Green cyclic arrows indicate protective/adaptive processes. Red upward arrows indicate increased oxidation/stress-related features. Simple directional arrows connect environment, response and outcome.

## 6. Palette / outline / text hierarchy
Natural rice greens and browns establish context. Red is reserved for stress/intensity cues, black for Cd, orange for the death outcome. Large bold labels such as Cell survival and Cell death dominate over smaller mechanism text.

## 7. Information density and evidence style
Information density is low-to-medium, with generous whitespace. The figure is intentionally conceptual and phenotype-centered rather than evidence-heavy.

## 8. Best reusable elements
- environmental context → cellular outcome flow;
- survival-versus-death branching;
- compact protective-response loop;
- simple stress-color semantics.

## 9. Suitable future use cases in Bath–RP project
Useful for graphical abstracts or presentation slides where an environmental perturbation needs to be connected quickly to a physiological outcome, for example microoxia/high-O2 stress, metal exposure, or maintenance-versus-collapse comparisons.

## 10. Weaknesses / what not to imitate
Avoid a clip-art look from overly literal icons. Do not overload the center with labels. Red arrows should remain reserved for one semantic role rather than becoming a generic emphasis device.
