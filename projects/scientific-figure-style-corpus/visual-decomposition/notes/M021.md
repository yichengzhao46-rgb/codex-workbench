## 2. Functional role

This figure explains a solar-driven methanotroph biohybrid for H2 production by first introducing the biological methane-to-biomass context, then the photocatalyst/redox module, and finally the complete integrated biohybrid mechanism. Its key communication task is **teaching an unfamiliar hybrid system in stages**.

## 3. Layout and reading logic

The design uses a clear `staged_a_b_c_story`:

- panel a: simplified biological context;
- panel b: isolated photocatalyst / sacrificial-agent redox concept;
- panel c: large integrated cell-scale mechanism.

The bottom panel receives most of the visual area, so the viewer understands that the upper modules are explanatory prerequisites rather than separate conclusions.

## 4. Cell / object rendering

The methanotroph is a large soft-edged cell capsule with warm translucent fills and a luminous outer halo. Molecular structures are simplified but explicit enough to convey photocatalyst chemistry. Hydrogenase is shown as a recognizable protein/structure motif rather than a generic box.

The figure therefore mixes editorial cell illustration with molecular and protein graphics without attempting strict scale consistency.

## 5. Arrow grammar

Black/gray arrows carry material and process flow. Yellow arrows are reserved for light/photoelectron transfer, giving photochemical steps a strong semantic accent. The final H2 product arrow is large and dark, creating a strong endpoint.

The figure also uses curved arrows to keep the mechanism visually contained within the cell rather than spreading the pathway across the entire page.

## 6. Palette / outline / typography

Warm yellow/cream dominates the solar/photoactivation layer. Teal/cyan is used for methane-carbon and photocatalyst structures. The palette is soft and coherent, with high saturation reserved for the light-driven electron step.

Large bold labels such as the input/output concepts act as visual anchors. Supporting mechanistic text is smaller and remains inside the cell.

## 7. Information density and evidence style

Density is medium. The figure is explanatory rather than data-integrated. It prioritizes intuitive mechanism learning over exhaustive molecular detail.

## 8. Best reusable elements

- small prerequisite modules above a large integrated mechanism
- warm light halo as a semantic field
- explicit photoelectron color coding
- large product endpoint
- cell-scale integration of molecule → electron → enzyme → product
- visually distinct chemical/molecular module before embedding it in the cell

## 9. Suitable future use cases in the Bath–RP project

Best suited to:

- Bath–Bio-Se biohybrid overview;
- explaining a semiconductor or Bio-Se photoelectron module before showing potential EEU into Bath;
- objective figures where Stage 1 establishes particle/material formation and Stage 2 tests light-driven electron use;
- any Bath-centered biohybrid whose chemistry would be hard to understand if introduced only inside the final cell schematic.

For a Bath–Bio-Se adaptation, panel a could establish methane-supported Bath physiology, panel b could explain Bio-Se photoexcitation/electron-hole generation, and panel c could integrate Bath + Bio-Se + light + candidate EEU while clearly marking the EEU step as hypothetical until tested.

## 10. Weaknesses / what not to imitate

The soft glow, translucent shell and BioRender-like rendering can become decorative if applied too heavily. The cell outline is generic rather than Bath-specific. Future Bath figures should preserve the staged storytelling but use a more species-specific spherical Bath morphology, restrained glow and explicit evidence-boundary notation for candidate EEU.
