# M014

## 1. Figure identity
- Canonical ID: M014
- Legacy ID: Z125
- Source type: local curated asset
- Visual class: membrane-to-metabolism methanotroph cutaway

## 2. Functional role
This figure organizes methane oxidation, electron transport, carbon assimilation and fermentation inside one readable methanotroph scaffold. It is especially useful because it links membrane-level oxidation to downstream carbon fate without becoming a full genome-scale pathway map.

## 3. Layout and reading logic
The figure reads top-to-bottom. Extracellular CH4/O2 enter at the top; methane oxidation and respiratory/electron-transfer modules occupy the membrane zone; methanol/formaldehyde form the central carbon node; assimilation and fermentation pathways branch into the lower cytoplasm.

## 4. Cell / object rendering
The whole-cell outline is schematic and subordinate to pathway organization. Membrane proteins and quinone/electron-transfer components are simplified functional icons. The lower cytoplasm is intentionally open so carbon branches remain legible.

## 5. Arrow grammar
Black/gray arrows carry most biochemical flow. Selected colored routes distinguish electron transfer, respiration and carbon branching. The strongest grammar is directional architecture rather than decorative arrow styling: membrane processes are physically above intracellular carbon allocation.

## 6. Palette / outline / text hierarchy
A pale blue background provides low visual noise. Salmon/pink membrane/protein modules contrast with restrained green/red pathway accents. Compartment placement and pathway grouping do more hierarchy work than font size alone.

## 7. Information density and evidence style
Density is medium-high but disciplined. The figure is mechanism-only rather than a multi-panel evidence figure. It succeeds because pathways are spatially grouped and the central carbon node is visually obvious.

## 8. Best reusable elements
- top-down membrane → carbon architecture;
- central formaldehyde/carbon node;
- separate respiration, fermentation and assimilation branches;
- membrane proteins drawn only where they change process interpretation;
- restrained compartment background.

## 9. Suitable future use cases in Bath–RP project
One of the strongest references for Bath metabolism, especially methane oxidation under oxygen limitation, carbon/electron allocation, donor-product formation and links between respiratory state and formate/acetate/H2 release.

## 10. Weaknesses / what not to imitate
Do not include every enzyme or thin arrow. Avoid presenting a conceptual metabolism map as direct species-resolved evidence. For Bath figures, keep the pathway set tightly aligned with the claims actually measured in the experiment.
