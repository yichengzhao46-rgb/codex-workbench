# M017

## 1. Figure identity

- Legacy ID: U003
- Journal: Environmental Science & Technology (2025)
- Figure: Visual Abstract
- Role in PR20: environmental-process / rhizosphere zoom reference

## 2. Functional role

This figure explains how a rice-root iron-plaque microenvironment mediates linked methane oxidation, iron cycling and nitrogen fixation. The figure's primary communication problem is **spatial localization**: the mechanism matters because it occurs at a specific root-associated interface.

## 3. Layout and reading logic

The composition begins with a full plant/root context on the left and then moves into a large rounded zoom panel on the right. Dashed connectors tie the enlarged mechanistic region back to the root zone.

Inside the inset, the root is the organizing axis. Fe(III)/Fe(II), oxygen, methane oxidation and nitrogen transformations are arranged around it rather than shown as a conventional linear pathway.

Reading order is:

1. whole-plant context;
2. root/plaque location;
3. local Fe redox cycle;
4. methanotroph / Fe-reducer interactions;
5. carbon and nitrogen outputs.

## 4. Cell / object rendering

The plant is rendered as a flat editorial illustration with low anatomical detail. Microbial cells are small stylized icons embedded within the zoom region. The root cross-section is simplified and used as a structural anchor rather than a biological illustration target.

The Fe plaque is represented through warm-toned mineral color and circular Fe(III)/Fe(II) cycle elements rather than realistic mineral textures.

## 5. Arrow grammar

Different elemental/process classes use distinct arrow colors. Carbon/methane flows, nitrogen transformations and oxygen/iron-related movements are visually separated. The arrows are bold enough to read over the pale inset background.

The dashed zoom connectors are semantically different from biochemical arrows and should remain visually lighter.

## 6. Palette / outline / typography

Natural green/brown colors provide environmental context. Red, blue and green process accents are then used for mechanistic flows. The overall palette is restrained enough that the arrows remain the strongest visual signals.

Typography is compact; some labels inside the inset are small, which is acceptable for a paper figure but less ideal for a presentation slide.

## 7. Information density and evidence style

Density is medium. The figure carries multiple biogeochemical processes but avoids full pathway detail. It acts as an environmental mechanism overview rather than a biochemical map.

## 8. Best reusable elements

- whole-environment → interface zoom
- root/mineral interface as central spatial scaffold
- dashed zoom connectors
- circular redox pair placed near the interface
- color-coded C/N/Fe process arrows
- low-saturation environmental backdrop with stronger mechanistic accents

## 9. Suitable future use cases in the Bath–RP project

Best suited to:

- environmental introduction figures showing where Bath–partner interactions may occur;
- methane/oxygen overlap zones in wetland, paddy or sediment interfaces;
- Bath–Bio-Se figures where a mineral or particle-rich microenvironment needs to be localized;
- figures that move from ecological context to a defined bottle-system hypothesis.

## 10. Weaknesses / what not to imitate

The zoom inset becomes visually busy when too many labels accumulate around the root. Do not copy the exact number of process colors. For Bath–RP, keep the environmental context simple and let only the most important CH4/O2/donor/carbon arrows use saturated accents.
