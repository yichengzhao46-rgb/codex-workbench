# M022

## 1. Figure identity

- Legacy ID: U008
- Journal: Environmental Science & Technology (2026)
- Figure: Visual Abstract
- DOI: 10.1021/acs.est.6c02194
- Role in PR20: adaptive-evolution / acclimation graphical-abstract reference

## 2. Functional role

The figure links a deliberate engineering intervention (targeted acclimation) to a long-term microbial adaptive response and then to functional gains. It is useful when a story must connect process conditioning, community evolution, metabolic rewiring and final biodegradation performance without becoming a full omics figure.

## 3. Layout and reading logic

The composition is split into two major vertical domains connected by a central arrow. The left side presents the engineering/acclimation sequence from prolonged exposure to enhanced biodegradation and phenotype. The right side presents the biological explanation: adaptive evolution, mobile genetic elements / virus-plasmid exchange, horizontal gene transfer, stress response, carbon metabolism and C-N catalytic functions. The two domains are visually balanced but conceptually asymmetric: intervention and outcome on the left, mechanistic explanation on the right.

## 4. Cell / object rendering

Cells and reactor/process objects are flat editorial icons with restrained pseudo-depth. The consortium is represented by a small community of simple microbial shapes rather than large detailed cells. Genetic-exchange elements are simplified as circular/linear plasmid or viral symbols. This keeps the visual emphasis on system adaptation rather than morphology.

## 5. Arrow grammar

A strong horizontal arrow connects targeted acclimation to adaptive evolution. Within each domain, smaller arrows connect exposure, biodegradation and phenotype or connect cells, mobile genetic elements, HGT and metabolic rewiring. Arrow hierarchy is therefore semantic: the large central arrow carries the paper-level causal story, while smaller local arrows carry submechanisms.

## 6. Palette / outline / text hierarchy

The figure uses a cool cyan/teal frame for the acclimation side and a warm peach/orange frame for the adaptive-evolution side. Section headers are large and colored to match their domain. Internal icons use low-saturation blues, oranges and greens with dark gray outlines. The paired cool-versus-warm palette makes the intervention-to-evolution transition immediately legible.

## 7. Information density and evidence style

Information density is medium. The figure compresses several mechanistic concepts into a small canvas but avoids gene-level detail. It functions as a graphical abstract: high-level process modules, not quantitative evidence panels.

## 8. Best reusable elements

- paired intervention-versus-adaptation domains;
- one dominant cross-panel causal arrow;
- top process labels plus bottom outcome labels;
- compact microbial-community icon rather than one hero cell;
- simple HGT / plasmid / virus motifs;
- cool-to-warm color transition to distinguish engineering input from biological response;
- bottom summary boxes for phenotype and metabolic rewiring.

## 9. Suitable future use cases in the Bath project

Useful for adaptation experiments, long-term oxygen or material acclimation, evolutionary/physiological rewiring, staged reactor conditioning, or any objective where a designed perturbation leads to a community-level mechanistic change and a functional phenotype. It could also guide a future figure linking repeated environmental selection to altered Bath donor metabolism or partner adaptation, provided such evolution is experimentally supported.

## 10. Weaknesses / what not to imitate

Do not use this grammar for a single-timepoint mechanistic pathway, because the paired domains imply a temporal or causal progression. Avoid adding dense omics result panels into the same canvas; that belongs to PR21. Avoid representing HGT or evolutionary change unless directly supported by the study design.
