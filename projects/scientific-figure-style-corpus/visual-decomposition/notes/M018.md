## 2. Functional role

The figure contrasts two Fe2+ regimes and connects them to different biological outcomes: one side emphasizes growth with an energy-intensive carbon-fixation route, while the other emphasizes activity with an alternative carbon-metabolism strategy. The visual problem is **condition-dependent functional reprogramming**.

## 3. Layout and reading logic

The canvas is divided into two matched vertical panels. Each panel uses the same top-to-bottom grammar:

1. external condition and cell population;
2. a small response curve summarizing the condition optimum;
3. downward transition into a larger cell cutaway;
4. internal pathway emphasis and phenotype label.

The left/right matching makes the comparison immediate. Color-coded dashed borders reinforce panel identity without requiring a heavy divider.

## 4. Cell / object rendering

Cells are soft 2.5D rounded capsules with slight transparency, gradient fill and glow. Internal mechanisms are deliberately schematic rather than anatomically precise. Molecules and pathway labels are surrounded by soft colored halos that make focal metabolites easy to find.

This rendering is more polished and presentation-oriented than a flat pathway diagram, while still remaining diagrammatic.

## 5. Arrow grammar

Arrows are relatively sparse. Curved colored arrows connect substrate/product labels and pathway modules. The figure relies more on spatial placement and pathway naming than on dense arrow networks.

A useful lesson is that a comparison figure can keep arrows minimal if the panel grammar itself already carries much of the meaning.

## 6. Palette / outline / typography

Orange is used for the growth/low-Fe side and blue for the activity/high-Fe side. The palette is therefore both semantic and structural. Cell fills stay pale cyan so that condition colors remain accents rather than overwhelming the object.

Typography is bold for the high-level phenotype labels and lighter/smaller for pathway names.

## 7. Information density and evidence style

Density is medium. The figure compresses a complex metabolic interpretation into a graphical-abstract level comparison. The small response curves provide a minimal evidence cue without turning the image into a data figure.

## 8. Best reusable elements

- mirrored condition panels with identical internal grammar
- phenotype word as the largest textual outcome
- tiny response-curve inset above each mechanism
- soft 2.5D cell capsule
- pathway switching shown by changing only a few internal labels/arrows
- restrained two-color condition coding

## 9. Suitable future use cases in the Bath–RP project

Best suited to:

- standard microoxic versus high-O2 perturbation;
- donor-rich versus donor-poor Bath output;
- comparing RP maintenance / carbon incorporation under two operational states;
- graphical abstracts where pathway switching is more important than detailed enzyme chemistry.

A Bath–RP adaptation could keep Bath/RP morphology constant across panels and use the condition color only for donor production, energy/redox state and inorganic-carbon incorporation.

## 10. Weaknesses / what not to imitate

The soft glow and heavy translucency can reduce crispness in print or at small size. Avoid using glow around every metabolite. For journal figures, retain the matched-panel composition but use cleaner edges and fewer decorative effects.
