# NEW001 — Volcanic environmental-system landscape

## Drawing idea
Build one continuous oblique landscape and let physical space replace panel boxes.

## Composition
Underground magma → volcano → eruption plume → upper-atmosphere transport → downwind mountains → rainfall/lahar → river → communities/water quality → ecosystem recovery.

## Hierarchy
1. Continuous terrain scaffold.
2. Major eruptive/transport processes.
3. Downstream environmental consequences.
4. Numbered labels and concise annotations.

## Object rendering
Editorial 2D–2.5D terrain, geometric cutaway surfaces, simplified modular trees/buildings/treatment objects, no photorealistic texture.

## Palette
Cool grey/grey-purple rock and ash, low-saturation green vegetation, cool blue water/rain, restrained coral-orange magma, pale gold radiation accents.

## Connector grammar
Use arrows only where movement is real: plume ascent, prevailing wind, deposition, runoff, lahar. Numbered process points guide reading without panel frames.

## Whitespace
Keep sky and valley corridors open for labels. Avoid filling every region with icons.

## Information density
Medium-high overall, but spatially distributed.

## Reusable rules
- Space itself can encode sequence.
- Warm colors reserved for focal energetic processes.
- One landscape can contain multiple process scales without explicit boxes.

## Best use
Environmental-process overviews, wetland/coastal context, pollution transport, ecological disturbance/recovery.

## Avoid transfer
Do not import terrain complexity into simple laboratory workflows.

## Prompt logic
First lock camera/background/landscape continuity; then define spatial route; then assign scientific processes to real locations; finally add semantic palette and labels.
